Chapter 01 — The Testing

Between 1946 and 1958, the United States detonated 67 nuclear weapons in the Marshall Islands.

The blasts were massive. Castle Bravo alone — detonated on March 1, 1954 — yielded 15 megatons, a thousand times more powerful than the bomb dropped on Hiroshima. The fallout contaminated islands, ocean, and people hundreds of miles away.

EN
Chapter 02 — The Displacement

Elevated cancer rates followed. Land became irradiated. Food was poisoned. Families fled the only home they'd ever known.

Many Marshallese scattered across the Pacific and eventually to the United States. Through mission work and community networks, a significant population found their way to an unlikely destination: Enid, Oklahoma — a small city in the heart of rural America, thousands of miles from the islands they once called home.

These are people who are overcoming trauma and building lives here — thousands of miles from a homeland they can never return to.

— Brandon Kobs, Producer

Timeline
1946
Operation Crossroads
The United States begins nuclear weapons testing at Bikini Atoll. Residents are relocated with promises they can return. They never will.
1954
Castle Bravo
The largest U.S. nuclear test detonates at 15 megatons. Radioactive fallout spreads across inhabited islands, contaminating people, water, and food for generations.
1958
Final Detonation
The 67th and final nuclear test. The cumulative radiation has devastated the environment and the health of the Marshallese people irreversibly.
1986
Compact of Free Association
Marshallese citizens gain the right to live and work in the United States. A diaspora begins as many seek healthcare and opportunity far from irradiated homelands.
2000s
A Community Takes Root
Through church networks and word of mouth, Marshallese families build new lives in Enid, Oklahoma — carrying their culture, their trauma, and their resilience.
2024
67 Bombs to Enid Premieres
After four years of filmmaking, the documentary premieres — winning Best Documentary at deadCenter and catching the attention of the United Nations.
The Film

An intimate portrait of survival, culture, and resilience

From Executive Producer Errol Morris, 67 Bombs to Enid is a character-driven documentary by Kevin Ford and Ty McMahan. What begins as a historical inquiry into the legacy of American nuclear testing becomes a deeply human portrait — of displacement and community, of trauma passed through generations, and of the extraordinary resilience of people building new lives in an unlikely place.
